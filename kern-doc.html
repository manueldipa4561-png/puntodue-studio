04 · The idea

Missions as experiments on yourself

A mission is a real task from a real company. It is also built to test one thing about you.

MISSION 1Build from zeroAn open brief. Tests energy in starting.
MISSION 2Improve what existsA closed task. Tests polish and finishing.
MISSION 3Work with someoneTests solo vs paired.
RESULTYour KernA hypothesis about how you work, with evidence.
Contrast, not volumeThe pattern between missions is the signal, not any single result.
Two kinds of evidenceWhat you did (finished, abandoned, restarted) plus what you said in your reflection.
Always a hypothesis“That feels right” or “Not really”. Never a diagnosis or personality type.
A sign for the next personAfter a mission you leave one short tip. No feed, no followers.
No clock, nothing lockedMissions show no completion time, because pace is personal and a stated time discourages hard attempts. Every mission is open to everyone immediately, and the 1 to 3 order is only a suggested path.
Many answers, judged on qualityAnyone can add several answers to one mission. The contest is who does it better, never who does it first. Who judges is still open.
yourKERNThe app section showing time spent on KERN missions (private, never a target), your interests, completed missions and suggested next ones, each with its evidence.

And older people have the same problem: about a third of employees want to leave their job but do not know what to do next (CVwizard 2025 survey, secondary). Keep 14 to 35 as the target audience for design and marketing, but do not block or push away older users.

Minors change the rulesAge of digital consent varies by country (from my memory, unverified: US under 13 is barred by COPPA; EU countries set 13 to 16, e.g. Italy 14, Germany 16, France 15; Quebec 14). Real company tasks, data on reflections, and any pay all need extra care under 18. A lawyer should confirm before any teen launch.

Recommended rollout

STAGE 1 · LAUNCH18 to 35Real partner tasks, can be paid, can consent.
STAGE 216 to 17Parental sign-off, KERN-vetted missions, no pay.
STAGE 314 to 15“Junior” track, school or parent-led, KERN-written missions only.

Why not launch with teens? The idea is untested, and a failed test on minors carries legal and reputational cost. Prove the loop with adults, then extend it. This is a sequencing call, not a rejection of your range.

06 · Challenges and answers

Where the idea was pushed

Companies won't post tasks for people with no direction

They post a normal small task. KERN wraps it with the experiment design. The company gets an output; KERN gets a signal.

One mission is just noise

Agreed. A Kern needs at least 3 contrasting missions. Earlier results are labelled “early”.

Cold start: where do the first missions come from?

KERN-written missions, Punto Due Studio's own clients as the first partner, and 2 or 3 small startups. Big names are already with Forage.

Why would a tech company bother?

They get outputs and a shortlist by working style instead of CV. Free for them at first; payment comes after early proof.

AI-generated insights will feel fake

Rules first, not AI. Every signal shows the answers behind it and can be rejected.

It becomes a marketplace or social network

Not built. The only social piece is the short tip per mission.

Signals from 3 missions may still be shallow

A signal needs behaviour plus self-report. It stays “early” until a contrast pair is complete. “Not really” lowers its weight.

09 · Validation

Test before building more

Run 3 missions by hand with 5 people over about 10 days. A form and a shared doc are enough. The test passes if:

Completion
at least 3 of 5 finish all three missions
The signal lands
at least 3 of 5 say “that feels right”
They pass it on
at least 2 of 5 recommend it unprompted

Miss two of three and the mission design changes before any more app code.

The name, and what protects it

DomainRegister kern.it now. Do not build the brand around it. Other domains are unchecked.
TrademarkNo filing yet. Clearance search (EU, US, Canada, in software, education and business services) before the first paying company, an app-store listing, or marketing spend.
The safer assetThe mark and palette outlive the word. If a same-field conflict appears, rename before spending.

12 · Growth and money

How KERN grows without becoming a chore

My proposals, none tested. They follow your rules: no clocks, nothing locked, quality over speed.

Dare a friendSend a mission link. You both answer, then see the two answers side by side. It fits the “who does it better” idea and needs no reward.
Share your Kern cardThe finished Trail ends in a card made to be posted. This is the main free loop.
Mission of the weekOne featured partner mission a week, with a public “best answers” page. Gives people a reason to return and a page others can find.
Groups and schoolsUniversities, bootcamps and communities run a mission together with a group board. One partner brings many users.
Referral rewards: cosmetic onlyReferring or daring earns things like extra cairn stones or card styles. Never ranking, never access, because the contest must stay about quality. No cash or gifts for under-18s.
People are always freeNo paywall for taking missions or seeing your Kern. Charging users would break “open to everyone”.

Decision: how “who does it better” is judged

Decided by me, from your rules. Untested, so it goes into the 5-person hand test.

1. Answers stay hidden until you submit your ownYou cannot copy what you have not seen. After you submit, the Board opens.
2. Anonymous peer review with a rubricEach person rates 3 random answers, author hidden, on three questions: found the real problem, explained the choice, usable by someone new. Reviewers cannot pick who they review, so friends cannot boost friends.
3. Rank is the rubric score, not popularityNo like counts. A rank appears only after at least 5 reviews. Random assignment means an early answer has no advantage, so speed never matters.
4. Reviewers earn weightPeople whose ratings tend to agree with the crowd and with partner picks count more. Careless or one-sided reviewers count less.
5. The partner picks the official bestThe company chooses its favourites with a one-line reason. That is the badge, and the only thing that can lead to a shortlist.
6. Improving an answer re-enters reviewRevise it and it is rated again. That rewards the struggle you described.
